About WORKAID

A versatile company specialising in consultation, business outsourcing, construction, renovation, restoration, interior design and maintenance services — catering to industrial and residential projects alike.

Who we are

One agency for
the building and
the business

Most owners end up managing a crowd — one contractor for the leak, another for the interiors, a consultant for the licences and an agency for the manpower. Nobody owns the outcome, and every problem becomes somebody else's problem.

WORKAID was built to remove that gap. On the technical side we handle maintenance, waterproofing, renovation and interior execution. On the business side we handle licensing, manpower, outsourcing, process and product work. Same company, same standards, one point of contact.

We work with a focus on innovation and excellence, so that every project is executed with precision and professionalism — which is a long way of saying we would rather find the cause than sell you a coat of paint.

How we operate

Five rules
we don't bend

These are not slogans — they are the reasons a client calls us back for the second project, which is the only metric that really counts.

Diagnose before quoting

We do not price a repair we have not understood. If the cause is not what you were told, we will say so — even when the smaller job is worth less to us.

Everything in writing

Scope, materials, brand names, timeline, exclusions, payment stages and warranty. A number on a scrap of paper is not a quotation.

One point of contact

A named person who knows your site and answers the phone — not a rotating queue of supervisors who each start the story again.

Finish the last 5%

The snag list is part of the job, not a favour. Handover means closed items, cleaned site and documents issued.

Supervised labour

Crews work under our engineers with documented compliance — not sub-let to whoever was available that week.

Say the difficult thing early

Delays, cost changes and bad news travel to you immediately. Surprises at the end are a failure of communication, not of construction.
