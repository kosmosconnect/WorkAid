Waterproofing

Choosing a waterproofing system that suits the surface

Membrane, liquid coating and crystalline systems each fail in different conditions. The right choice depends on exposure, foot traffic and what is underneath.

  • Terraces with traffic need protection over the membrane
  • Basements need negative-side systems, not surface coatings
  • Ask for the product datasheet before approving a quote

Reading a damp patch: where the water actually came from

The stain is rarely above the source. Before agreeing to re-coat anything, rule out the four usual culprits.

  • Blocked or undersized rainwater outlets
  • Untreated parapet, expansion and construction joints
  • Leaking concealed plumbing behind wet walls
  • Rising damp from an unsealed plinth

Maintenance

What a good AMC actually covers

Annual maintenance contracts vary wildly. A useful one names the assets, the visit frequency and the response time in writing.

  • An asset register, not a vague scope line
  • Stated visit frequency and a signed service log
  • Response SLA for breakdowns, and what is excluded

Interiors

Planning a renovation you have to live through

Occupied-site renovation is a sequencing problem more than a construction one. Decide the order before the first hammer.

  • Finish wet areas and services first, finishes last
  • Order long-lead items before demolition starts
  • Agree dust control and daily clean-up in the contract

Compliance

The MSME document set worth keeping current

Most compliance panic is really a filing problem. Keep these current and tender paperwork stops being an emergency.

  • Udyam registration with details that match your GST
  • Trade, factory and pollution consents with renewal dates
  • Labour records if you engage contract manpower

Operations

Mapping a process before trying to improve it

Write down what happens today, including the workarounds. Improvement projects fail when they optimise the version nobody actually follows.

  • Map the real path, including the informal steps
  • Measure wait time, not just work time
  • Write the SOP with the people who do the work

Before the monsoon, on a dry substrate. Work done on damp concrete traps moisture under the system and fails early. If a leak appears mid-monsoon, we do a temporary containment and schedule the permanent treatment for the dry window.

A correctly specified and correctly laid system on a sound substrate should give many years of service; the number depends on the product, the exposure and the traffic. Ask for the warranty in writing with the product name on it — a verbal figure is worth nothing.

Repair is cheaper only when the existing system is sound and the failure is local — a joint, an outlet, a crack. When the membrane has debonded across the slab, patching repeatedly costs more over three years than one correct re-lay.
